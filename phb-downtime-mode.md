# Downtime Mode

Drop-in text, then the design notes. Kept deliberately short: this is a combat playtest, and Downtime
is the mode your table will use least. Most of what belongs here already exists in Chapter 5, so the
section's real job is to be a hub rather than a second copy.

---

# The drop-in text

> ## Downtime Mode
>
> Every mode of play spends a different currency. In **Encounter Mode** you spend actions. In
> **Exploration Mode** you spend minutes and hours. In **Downtime Mode** you spend **days**, and the
> GM tells you how many you have before the world moves again.
>
> **One activity per day.** Each day of downtime, each character chooses one Downtime Activity and
> pursues it. An activity that takes a week is simply the same choice made on seven consecutive days,
> and interrupting it loses the days already spent unless the GM says otherwise.
>
> Ordinary rest is not a Downtime Activity. A night's sleep already restores your Hit Points and
> clears **Wounded**; Downtime is for the things a night cannot fix.
>
> ### Downtime Activities
>
> | Activity | Time | What it does |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Train** | 1 week | Work with a teacher, a rival, or a manual. That Constellation becomes **Flared**. This is the deliberate path to growth you did not stumble into at the table. |
> | **Retrain** | 1 month | Move 1 already spent Talent Point anywhere legal. Prerequisites and Proficiency Rank gates still apply. |
> | **Earn a Living** | 1 week | Practise a trade. Roll a relevant Skill or Lore check against a Threshold the GM sets. |
> | **Research** | 1 week | A Lore check to learn something the world knows and you do not. |
> | **Anything else** | varies | Carouse, court, scheme, build, recruit. The GM sets the check and the stakes. |
>
> *Crafting and repair arrive with the Crafting Constellation in a later packet.*
>
> ### Earning a Living
>
> What a week of honest work is worth depends on how good you are at it.
>
> | Your Proficiency Rank | A week's earnings |
> |---|---|
> | Trained | 2 gp |
> | Expert | 5 gp |
> | Master | 10 gp |
> | Legendary | 20 gp |
>
> **Critical success:** earn as though you were one rank higher. **Success:** as listed. **Failure:**
> half, and you have scraped by. **Critical failure:** nothing, and something has gone wrong that will
> want dealing with.
>
> ### Training and the Flare
>
> **You may benefit from Train only once between Milestones.** A week in the yard can point your growth
> where you want it, but it cannot replace the growth your play earned. If it could, nobody would ever
> need to Flare a Constellation the hard way, and the third Axiom would be decoration.
>
> ### How much Downtime you get
>
> The GM decides, and should say so plainly at the start of it: *"You have three weeks before the
> thaw."* Downtime is a length of rope, not an open field. It is the pressure of the calendar that
> makes choosing between Retraining and Earning a Living an actual choice.

# Why it is built this way

**The section is a hub, not a chapter.** Train and Retrain already have rules in Chapter 5 under
*Flares* and *Retraining*. Restating them here in different words is how two versions of a rule end up
in one book. The table above should cross-reference rather than redefine, and the numbers should live
in exactly one place. I would put them here, since this is where a player looks when they ask "what
can I do with three weeks," and leave Chapter 5 pointing this way.

**The one-week Train limit is the important line.** Right now Chapter 5 says a week of downtime
training Flares a Constellation, with no cap. Uncapped, that quietly dismantles your third Axiom: given
a month of downtime a player Flares whatever they like, four times, and "earned, not scheduled" becomes
"scheduled." Once between Milestones keeps the escape hatch open, since a player who never rolled a
crit in the Constellation they care about is not stuck, while leaving most growth where the Axiom
wants it. **This is a new restriction and it is the one decision in this section worth arguing about.**

**Earning is priced against your armour economy.** A Trained character earns 2 gp a week, so roughly 8
gp a month. A full mail harness is 25 gp and a plate one 45, which means honest work buys plate in
something like a year, and adventuring buys it in an afternoon. That gap should be visible: it is the
reason the characters are out there.

**The currency framing does real teaching work.** Actions, then minutes, then days is one sentence that
makes the three Modes feel like one system with a zoom control rather than three unrelated rulesets.

# Noted for the packet that adds Crafting

*Craft or Repair* is cut from the table above rather than fudged, because nothing in this playtest can
test it: there is no Crafting Constellation to roll and no rule that damages equipment. When it
arrives, the row is:

> | **Craft or Repair** | 1 week | Make or mend equipment with Crafting, the right tools, and materials. Materials cost half the item's price. |

Two things that will want deciding at the same time. **Whether armour can be damaged at all**, since
Repair has nothing to do until it can, and a per-Zone armour system is an unusually good fit for
degradation if you ever want it. And **whether Crafting overlaps trade Lores**: Lore is already a
template with one copy per subject, so Lore (Smithing) and Crafting will both plausibly claim the
forge unless you draw the line deliberately. The cleanest split is usually that Lore knows about it
and Crafting makes it.
