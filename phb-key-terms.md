# Key Terms

Drop-in copy for the Key Terms section. Your 13 terms are all here, in your order and grouping, plus
27 more that the book already leans on in multiple chapters. Every definition is one to three
sentences and ends with where the full rule lives, so this section stays a lookup and never becomes a
second rulebook.

---

**Assumptions.** Some terms and concepts in this book assume you are already familiar with the latest
tabletop roleplaying games, like Dungeons & Dragons and Pathfinder. These terms, such as Skill,
Level, Check, Initiative, Bonus, Penalty, and Round, are not reproduced here.

STARWROUGHT has a number of important terms, used in multiple places and listed here for clarity and
reference.

---

## Who You Are

**Ancestry.** The body you were born in: dwarf, elf, gnome, halfling, human. Your ancestry sets your
Hit Points per level, your Size, your Speed, and one special ability, and it forms the trunk of your
Origin Constellation. *(Ch. 3)*

**Background.** What happened to you before you took up this life. A trade, a shipwreck, a debt, a
year you cannot account for. Your background grants 1 Lore point and 2 Skill points. *(Ch. 3)*

**Bloodline.** The lineage inside your ancestry: a starlit elf, a forge blood dwarf, a torchbearer
human. Your bloodline adds a branch to your Origin Constellation and one more special ability.
*(Ch. 3)*

**Calling.** What you do about all of it. Your adventuring identity and the home of your signature
technique, whether that is a rage, a sneak attack, or a hunter's mark. You choose one at 1st level,
and you may open at most one new Calling per level after that. *(Ch. 3)*

**Culture.** Where you were raised. Your culture adds a branch to your Origin Constellation and
grants your starting languages. *(Ch. 3)*

**Origin Constellation.** The one constellation your ancestry, bloodline, and culture merge into:
your history, drawn as a single sky. Its roots are granted free by the choices themselves, so your
Origin is already three points deep before you spend anything. *(Ch. 3, Ch. 6)*

## Attributes

**Attribute.** One of four measures of what your character is made of. You never buy attribute points
and you never roll them. Every constellation feeds one attribute, and your bonus is the points you
have spent in its constellations divided by 3, rounded down, to a maximum of +5. *(Ch. 2)*

**Might.** Muscle, endurance, and raw force. Keys Endure, and adds to your damage with most weapons.

**Agility.** Speed, balance, and precision. Keys Evade.

**Wits.** Perception, memory, and fast thinking. Keys Awareness.

**Presence.** Raw nerve, willpower, and weight of personality. Keys Guard.

## How You Are Built

**Talent.** One thing your character can do, or one way they are better at doing it. Every talent
costs exactly 1 talent point, from the humblest to the most legendary. What separates them is not
price but what you must already be to reach them. *(Ch. 6)*

**Constellation.** A group of related talents, and the unit this game uses instead of skills,
classes, and feat lists. Your total investment in a constellation sets your proficiency rank in it,
and that rank unlocks its deeper tiers. *(Ch. 6)*

**Trait.** A word attached to an action, a talent, a weapon, or a piece of gear that plugs it into a
rule written somewhere else. Traits keep the game from repeating itself. A trait is never flavor: if
the word is in a trait line, some rule cares about it. *(Ch. 2; weapon traits in Ch. 4; action traits
in Ch. 7)*

**Talent Point.** The only build currency in STARWROUGHT. There are no stat arrays, no skill
increases, and no feat slots. Points are never banked: you spend one the moment you get it. *(Ch. 2)*

**Root.** The first talent of a constellation. Buying the root is what makes you Trained in it, and
nothing else in a constellation can be bought before its root. Identity roots, from your ancestry,
bloodline, and culture, are granted free. *(Ch. 6)*

**Tier.** The proficiency rank a talent demands before you may buy it, marked T, E, M, or L. An
Expert talent needs Expert rank. *(Ch. 6)*

**Proficiency.** How practiced your character is at a kind of task. It is a single number added to
every roll a constellation governs, and Untrained is simply +0 rather than a different rule.
*(Ch. 2)*

**Proficiency Rank.** Your standing in one constellation: Untrained +0, Trained +4, Expert +7, Master
+10, Legendary +13. Rising requires both points in that constellation (1, 4, 9, 16) and character
level (1, 5, 13, 19). Buying talents *is* how you advance; there is no separate increase to spend.
*(Ch. 2)*

**Flare.** A constellation is **Flared** when a critical success or a critical failure on a roll with
consequences, related to a talent inside it, lights it up. That is the only trigger, and any roll at
the table can do it, yours or someone else's. Milestone points may only be spent in a Flared
constellation, which is what makes your growth follow your deeds. *(Ch. 5)*

**Milestone.** A quarter of a level. Four milestones make a level, and the first three each grant 1
talent point, spent at once in a Flared constellation. *(Ch. 5)*

**Comet.** A talent point with no strings attached. Spend a Comet in any constellation you qualify
for, Flared or not. You begin play with three. *(Ch. 3, Ch. 5)*

**Retraining.** A week of downtime moves 1 already spent point anywhere legal, prerequisites and tier
gates still applying. Characters are maps of what they have done, and maps get redrawn. *(Ch. 5)*

**Hero Point.** You start each session with 1, to a maximum of 3, and the GM awards more for heroism,
clever play, and beautiful failure. Spend 1 to reroll a check you are Trained or better in. Spend all
of them to survive something that would have killed you. *(Ch. 2)*

## Rolling Dice

**Threshold.** The number a roll is measured against. STARWROUGHT uses one word where other games use
two, because AC and DC are the same idea seen from opposite ends. Every Defense you own is a
Threshold when something comes at you and a check when you answer it. *(Ch. 2)*

**Modes of Play.** **Encounter Mode** is when seconds matter and turns are counted. **Exploration
Mode** runs in minutes and hours. **Downtime Mode** runs in days, weeks, and months, and is where
income and retraining happen. *(Ch. 2)*

## Fighting

**Defense.** One of four constellations every character begins play Trained in. Each works two ways:
roll it to answer what comes at you, and it is the Threshold others must beat when they come. *(Ch. 2,
Ch. 6)*

**Awareness.** *Don't be surprised.* Wits. The check against Sneak, Feint, Lie, illusions, and
initiative. It never answers a blow; trickery is its business.

**Evade.** *Don't be there.* Agility. Answers a physical attack by slipping aside, and answers
blasts, traps, and collapsing floors.

**Guard.** *Don't let it through.* Presence. Answers a physical attack by meeting it with weapon,
shield, or braced arm, and answers fear, charm, and confusion.

**Endure.** *Survive what does come through.* Might. The check against poison, disease, exhaustion,
and dying. It never answers a blow, because it answers what no defense could have stopped.

**Weapons.** The constellation every character is Trained in at 1st level. It supplies the
proficiency for every attack roll you make. Your combat style supplies the attribute. *(Ch. 6)*

**Strike.** The basic attack action, and worth one, two, or three of your actions. Spending more buys
control over where the blow lands. *(Ch. 7)*

**Graze.** The attack result that lands where other games would say "miss." A Graze deals a single
weapon die and nothing else, then subtracts the Protection of the zone it found, and the defender
pays the Graze cost. Roughly two blows in five land here, which is why armor matters more than
dodging looks. *(Ch. 7)*

**Graze Cost.** What a Graze takes out of the defender even though the defense mostly worked: one of
your zones becomes Exposed. A defense that mostly works still moves you out of line. *(Ch. 7)*

**Zone.** One of the four parts of a body: **Head, Torso, Arms, Legs.** Each carries its own armor and
its own Protection. Ordinary Hits go to the torso, so most rounds you will use one number; the other
three matter once somebody has earned an opening. *(Ch. 7)*

**Exposed [zone].** That part of you is open, whether the armor there is displaced or your guard no
longer covers it. **That zone's Protection counts as 0**, an ordinary Hit may be placed there instead
of the torso, and it stays Exposed until you Recenter. *(Ch. 7)*

**Recenter.** One action, *concentrate*: gather yourself and clear every Exposed zone on you. *(Ch. 7)*

**Protection.** How much of a blow your body does not feel. Armor never makes you harder to hit; it
subtracts from the damage of everything that reaches the zone it covers. *(Ch. 4, Ch. 7)*

**Load.** The weight and stiffness of what you are wearing. *(Ch. 4)*

**Load Strain.** The Load of all your armor minus your Might, minimum 0. Subtract it from your Evade
and from any Might or Agility skill check. Training in Endure reduces it as your rank rises. *(Ch. 4)*

**Posture.** An optional reaction when you answer a blow: a way of throwing yourself into it that
buys a better result and costs you an Exposed zone whether or not it works. One posture per round,
however many blows come at you. *(Ch. 7)*

**Stance.** A way of standing and holding yourself, entered with an action and held until you leave
it, fall unconscious, or enter another. You can be in only one at a time. Combat styles are where
stances live. *(Ch. 7)*

**Off-guard.** A −2 circumstance penalty to Evade and Guard. This is the condition sneak attacks and
openings care about. *(Ch. 7)*

**Multiple Attack Penalty.** Your first attack each turn is unpenalized, your second takes −5, and
your third and beyond take −10. Any action with the *attack* trait counts toward it and suffers it.
*(Ch. 7)*

**Weapon Specialization.** Extra damage from your Weapons rank alone: +2 at Expert, +3 at Master, +4
at Legendary. It never applies to a Graze. *(Ch. 4)*

# Notes on the list

## Two I left out on purpose

**Panache** appears fourteen times but only inside Bravado, so it belongs in that Calling's entry
rather than in a book-wide glossary. Same reasoning for Prey and Rage. The test I applied is your
own: a term earns a Key Terms slot when it is used in **multiple places**, not merely used often.

**Degrees of success** stayed out because your Assumptions paragraph already covers Check, and a
Pathfinder player knows the four bands. Graze is the exception, and it has its own entry, because it
is the one band whose meaning STARWROUGHT changed.

## Worth adding to the Assumptions list

Action, Turn, Speed, Reach, Hit Points, Damage, Condition, Range, Area, Cover, Difficult Terrain, GM.
All of them arrive from other games intact, and naming them saves a reader from hunting for
definitions that were never going to be here.

## Three places the book now disagrees with itself

Found while checking every definition against the rules text. All three concern the same change, so
they were probably one edit that did not finish.

**1. Who chooses the Exposed zone on a Graze.** Ch. 7 currently says a zone of your choice becomes
Exposed regardless of which Defense you used. That was the version the simulation flagged as a
loophole: you name the zone you care least about, and the Graze costs you nothing. The tell was
Recenter usage collapsing to 0.16 per round, because nothing worth protecting ever opened. Letting
your **method dictate** the zone when you Guard, so a shield fighter's Arms open and a braced
great weapon fighter's Legs open, measured 2.5 points of spread better and cannot be gamed. It also
gives all four zones a build reason to exist, which was the point of the change. Evade should keep
the free choice; you did twist away, and you decide what you left open.

**2. Both free postures now read "a zone of your choice."** Set Your Feet should open your **guard
zone**, or it is Give Ground with a different name.

**3. Postures cost a reaction in the rules text, including the free ones.** Pricing the basic posture
in reactions makes it dead text for exactly the players who invested in defending, since Shield
Block, Reactive Shield, Cover the Angle, and Opportune Parry all want the same reaction. The version
that tested well charges reactions for the **bought** postures only.

Also, two small ones: the Conditions in Brief entry for Exposed omits "that zone's Protection counts
as 0," which is the clause that gives Exposed its teeth (dropping it cost 8 points of spread). And
Ch. 7 reads "or you guard no longer covers it" where it wants "your guard."
