# Example of Play

Drop-in copy for the PHB. Format follows the Pathfinder model: player dialogue in rows, shaded boxes
explaining the rule as it comes up. Every number below is computed from v2.1 as printed, so the math
checks. Layout note for Word: dialogue rows as a two-column borderless table (speaker, line), boxes
as shaded single-cell tables.

The encounter is the one from the opening fiction, which means a reader who started at page one
already knows these three and wants to see how it ends.

---

*Dana plays **Hrolda**, a dwarf Berserker who fights behind a shield. Marco plays **Wren**, a human
Ambusher with a dagger in each hand. Priya plays **Kessa**, an elf who hunts with a greataxe. Mike is
the GM. All three are 1st level. They have cornered the Seamfinder in the stonecutters' quarter.*

---

**Mike:** Everyone roll initiative, and roll whatever you were actually doing. Wren, you were creeping along the wall, so that is Stealth. Kessa, Hrolda, you were both watching the stair, so Awareness.

> **Initiative is whatever you were already doing.** There is no separate initiative statistic in
> STARWROUGHT. You roll the check that matches the activity you were engaged in, which rewards the
> character who was doing the useful thing. Wren rolls Stealth and gets 25. Kessa rolls Awareness for
> 19. Hrolda rolls a 6 on the die for a total of 12. Mike gives the Seamfinder a 15 and writes the
> order down.

**Mike:** Wren first, then Kessa, then the thing, then Hrolda. Wren, it still has no idea you are there.

**Wren (Marco):** Which is how I like it. It hasn't acted yet, so Surprise Attacker means it's off-guard to me either way. First action, Strike with my right-hand dagger.

**Mike:** Roll Weapons against whichever Defense it answers with. It tries to slip aside, so that's its Evade.

> **You always roll.** Wren's attack is d20 plus her level, plus the attribute her combat style uses,
> plus her Weapons proficiency: 1 + 2 Agility + 4 Trained, so d20+7. Her daggers have **finesse**, which
> is why Agility replaces Might on the roll. She gets 13 for a total of 20. The Seamfinder's Evade
> Threshold is 16, reduced to 14 because being unseen makes a creature **off-guard** to you. She beats
> it, but not by 10, so it is a **Hit**.

**Mike:** That's a hit. Damage.

**Wren (Marco):** 1d4 for the dagger plus 1 Might, and 1d6 Sneak Attack because it's off-guard. Three, one, and four. Nine.

**Mike:** Its Torso soaks 2 of that. Six gets through. The blade goes in above the hip and the thing makes a sound like grinding stone.

> **A Hit lands on the torso** unless a zone has already been opened, and every zone subtracts its own
> **Protection** from the damage. Armor in STARWROUGHT never makes anything harder to hit. It decides
> how much of the blow the body actually feels.

**Wren (Marco):** Second action, left-hand dagger.

> **The multiple attack penalty** is −5 on your second attack in a turn and −10 on your third, but
> Wren's daggers are **agile**, so hers are −4 and −8. Her roll is d20+3 this time. She gets 8 for a
> total of 11 against that same 14.

**Mike:** Eleven. You miss it by three, so that's a Graze.

**Wren (Marco):** Still something. One weapon die, so 1d4. Three.

**Mike:** Minus 2 Protection again, so a single point. But it snatches its arm back out of the way to make even that much not land, and that opens it up. Its Arms are Exposed.

> **A Graze is what other games call a miss.** You missed by less than 10, so you still scored one
> weapon die and nothing else: no Might, no Sneak Attack, no extra dice from level. Then the
> defender pays the **Graze cost**, and one of their zones becomes **Exposed**. That is the important
> half. Even Wren's worst swing has cost the Seamfinder something, and Kessa is about to spend it.

**Wren (Marco):** Last action I Step back out of its reach.

---

**Kessa (Priya):** My turn. First action, Mark Prey on it.

**Mike:** Done. It's your prey until you Mark something else.

**Kessa (Priya):** Second action, I enter Overreach. I want the damage and I'll live with the Evade.

> **Stances** are entered with an action and held until you leave them. You can only be in one at a
> time. **Overreach** adds +2 damage to Kessa's Hits and Critical Hits, and drops her Evade by 2 for as
> long as she stays in it. Marking her prey adds 1d8 to her first Hit against it each round. Both are
> talent roots, bought with her very first points.

**Kessa (Priya):** And my last action is a Strike. Greataxe. d20+7.

**Mike:** It's hurt and it stops trying to be clever. It meets this one, so roll against its Guard.

**Kessa (Priya):** Seventeen on the die. Twenty-four.

**Mike:** Its Guard Threshold is 13, and it's still off-guard, so 11. You beat it by thirteen. Critical hit, and you pick the zone.

**Kessa (Priya):** Its arms are already open. There.

> **Beating the Threshold by 10 or more is a critical hit**: double damage, and the attacker chooses
> where it lands freely. Kessa chooses the **Exposed** Arms, and an Exposed zone counts its Protection
> as **0**. Her damage is 1d12 greataxe plus 2 Might plus 2 from Overreach plus 1d8 from Mark Prey. She
> rolls 9 and 5, for 18, doubled to **36**, and none of it is stopped. Wren's throwaway second dagger
> made this possible two dialogue lines ago.

**Mike:** The axe takes the arm off at the shoulder and the arm keeps moving after it lands. Also, Priya, that crit came off a Great Weapon Fighting talent. Great Weapon Fighting just Flared.

**Kessa (Priya):** Noted. That's where my next point goes.

> **This is the whole engine.** A critical success or critical failure on a roll with consequences,
> related to a talent in a constellation, makes that constellation **Flared**. Milestone points can
> only be spent in a Flared constellation, so a character grows along the lines their play actually
> traced. Kessa did not plan to become a great weapon specialist. She just did something with an axe
> that will be worth telling.

---

**Mike:** It doesn't seem to notice the arm. It comes at Kessa, and it reaches ten feet. Its Threshold is 16. Evade or Guard?

> **The GM does not roll attacks.** The creature has an attack **Threshold**, and the player rolls a
> Defense against it. Nothing about the odds changes; what changes is whose hand the die is in when
> something is about to happen to your character.
>
> | Your Defense roll vs the attack's Threshold | Result |
> |---|---|
> | Beat it by 10+ | **Miss.** Nothing touches you |
> | Meet or beat it | **Graze.** One weapon die, then Protection, and you pay the Graze cost |
> | Miss by less than 10 | **Hit.** Full damage |
> | Miss by 10+ | **Critical hit.** Double damage, attacker's choice of zone |

**Kessa (Priya):** My Evade is normally d20+6, but Overreach has it at d20+4, and my Guard is d20+6. So I brace on the axe haft and Guard. Nine on the die. Fifteen.

**Mike:** One short. Fifteen against sixteen is a Hit, full damage. Ten of it.

**Kessa (Priya):** I'm in a gambeson. That's 1 Protection.

**Mike:** It rakes rather than punches, so slashing, and padded turns slashing badly. Your gambeson is 0 against this. Take all ten.

> **Overreach's cost just arrived.** Without it her Evade would have been d20+6, and a 9 would have
> been 15 either way, so the stance did not decide this one; but it did take the better of her two
> Defenses off the table before she chose. **Materials matter too:** a piece's Protection drops by 1
> against the damage type its material turns poorly. Padded and leather turn slashing poorly, mail
> turns piercing poorly, plate turns bludgeoning poorly. Ten damage of Kessa's nineteen Hit Points is
> what a gambeson is worth against a claw.
>
> She could have taken a **posture**, spending a reaction for +2 at the price of a zone opening whether
> or not it worked. She declined, and said afterward that with the thing nearly dead she did not want
> to hand it a way in. That is the decision postures exist to create.

**Mike:** It's still going, and it swings back at Hrolda with what's left. Second attack this turn, so its Threshold drops to 11.

> **The multiple attack penalty works on creatures too**, and player-facing rolls make it visible in a
> pleasant way: instead of the GM subtracting 5 from a die roll nobody sees, the number the player
> needs simply gets easier. A monster's second swing is a worse swing, and you can watch it happen.

**Hrolda (Dana):** No shield up yet, I'm last in the order. I'll meet it on the axe haft anyway. Guard, d20+6. Twelve on the die, so eighteen.

**Mike:** Eighteen against eleven. You beat it, so that's a Graze. One die, 1d8, I get a four.

**Hrolda (Dana):** Mail shirt. Three Protection.

**Mike:** It's a rake, and mail turns slashing fine. So the full 3 applies and you take one. But you paid to do it. Pick a zone.

**Hrolda (Dana):** I braced low, so my legs. Legs Exposed.

> **Grazing is not free.** Hrolda kept almost all of the blow off herself and it still cost her an open
> zone. Note the difference between the two defenders this round: Kessa's better roll would have been a
> Graze and she took a full Hit instead by one point, while Hrolda's Graze cost her one Hit Point and
> a Legs zone. Armor decides what a Graze is worth; the die decides which band you land in.

---

**Hrolda (Dana):** My turn, and my legs are hanging open. Recenter first.

**Mike:** Legs closed. Two actions left.

> **Recenter ◆** clears every Exposed zone on you. It is worth noticing what just happened to Hrolda's
> turn: one Graze against her cost a third of it. That is the tempo price of being opened, and it is
> why Recenter is a real decision rather than free bookkeeping.

**Hrolda (Dana):** Raise my shield, and Strike with the battleaxe. d20+7. Six on the die. Thirteen.

**Mike:** It slips aside from that one, Evade 16, and it isn't off-guard to you the way it was to Wren. Thirteen misses by three. Graze.

**Hrolda (Dana):** 1d8. Five, minus 2 Protection. Three.

**Mike:** And it pays for that too. Another zone opens on it. The axe skids off the shoulder it has left and the thing backs into the mortar line, and for the first time in three nights it is the one giving ground.

That is the end of the first round. The second begins immediately, in the same order. The Seamfinder has taken 46 damage of its 60, it is down an arm, it has two zones hanging open, and it is still between the three of them and the stair.

# Two gaps this surfaced

Writing the example is a decent test of the rules, and it caught two things the book does not say.

**Nothing states how a creature's Threshold handles its own multiple attack penalty.** Player-facing
rolls mean the penalty cannot be applied to a die roll, so it has to come off the Threshold. I used
−5 and −10, mirroring the player rule, and it works nicely at the table because the second swing
visibly needs less from the defender. But it needs one sentence in Ch. 7, or every GM will invent
their own answer.

**Sweep is dead in a single-target fight**, which is most fights. "+1 circumstance bonus to attack a
target you have not yet attacked this turn" does nothing on your first attack against your only
enemy, and both the battleaxe and the greataxe carry it. Consider "+1 on your first attack against
each target," which reads the same in a crowd and actually pays out in a duel.
