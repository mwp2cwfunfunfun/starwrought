# Four follow-ups: reach, Heave, Squeeze, and the Example of Play

---

# SW-077: the new weapon Reach values

Shrinking Medium from 5 ft to 3 ft moves the half-space from 2.5 ft to 1.5 ft, so **every weapon's
Reach rises by exactly 1 foot.** The whole table shifts one notch; nothing needs re-deriving.

| Weapon | v2.5 | **New** |
|---|:-:|:-:|
| Unarmed, dagger | Adjacent | **2 ft** |
| Shortsword, hatchet | 2 | **2 ft** |
| Mace, club, battleaxe, warhammer | 2 | **3 ft** |
| Longsword, quarterstaff | 3 | **4 ft** |
| Rapier | 4 | **4 ft** |
| Greatsword, greataxe, maul | 4 | **5 ft** |
| Spear | 6 | **7 ft** |
| Polearm, halberd | 7 | **8 ft** |
| Pike, longspear | 12 | **14 ft** |

Unarmed and dagger land on **2 ft**, which matches your Medium Natural Reach exactly. That is the
right answer: a knife adds nothing to your arm, so a dagger fighter reaches precisely as far as a bare
fist. It also means **"Adjacent" disappears as a weapon Reach**, which is fine, because **Close** is a
trait on the weapon rather than a reach threshold and keeps working untouched.

## The consequence you will feel at the table

**The front line is 40% thinner than it was.** A Medium defender is 3 feet deep, not 5, so reaching
the ally behind them now takes Reach 3 rather than Reach 5.

| Reaches past a front-liner? | |
|---|---|
| **No** | unarmed, dagger, shortsword, hatchet |
| **Yes** | everything else, including a mace |

In v2.5 a longsword could not do this and a spear could, which made "pick up something longer" a real
escalation. Now almost every weapon can, and the escalation is gone.

**I would not fix this with numbers, because the system already prices it.** A creature between you
and your target grants them **Lesser Cover**, +1 to their Defense. So reaching past the front line is
possible for most weapons and costs you accuracy, while a shield wall of daggers genuinely cannot be
reached past at all. That is a decent equilibrium and it needs no new rule, only a sentence in the
reach section pointing at it:

> **Reaching past someone.** A creature standing between you and your target gives that target Lesser
> Cover. You can strike over a front line with most weapons; you will just be doing it at a penalty.

**One thing to check while you are in there.** Large is now 4 ft × 4 ft against Medium's 3 ft × 3 ft,
a difference of one foot. Every other edition makes Large roughly double. This is more realistic for
floor space, but it means Large creatures are barely more imposing than a person: the same number of
attackers can surround them, they squeeze through nearly the same gaps, and their Natural Reach of 3 ft
is the only thing marking them out. Deliberate or drift is worth knowing.

# SW-082: Heave, and how it differs from everything else

The fix is to **take the Stride out of Heave entirely.** Right now Heave is "Stride, then Shove," which
is word-for-word what *Bull Rush* does, and the movement is also what makes it feel like Haul. Remove
it and all five tools separate cleanly.

> **Shove ◆ to ◆◆** *(attack)*
>
> **◆** Push the target **straight away from you**, 3 feet, or 6 on a critical success. You may Stride
> after it as part of this action if it is in the same direction.
>
> **◆◆ Heave.** You set your feet and put your weight behind it. **You do not move.** Push the target
> **6 feet in any direction you choose**, or 12 on a critical success. You may not push them into or
> through your own space.

| | Do *you* move? | Where do *they* end up? |
|---|---|---|
| **Shove ◆** | Optionally, straight after them | Straight away from you |
| **Heave ◆◆** | **No** | **Anywhere you choose**, out of your reach |
| **Reposition ◆** | No | Anywhere, but still **within your reach** |
| **Haul ◆◆** | **Yes** | **With you** |
| **Bull Rush** (talent) | Yes | Pushed away, with momentum |

Heave against Reposition is now the sharpest pair in the set: **Heave sends them away, Reposition keeps
them.** One is for getting somebody off your archer, the other is for holding them where you want them.

And direction is what makes Heave worth two actions, because your own rules already give it teeth:
forced movement can take a creature off a ledge, stops at obstacles for damage, and ignores difficult
terrain. Heave is the tool that puts someone into the brazier, into the wall, or into your pikeman's
threatened ring. Deliberately worthless in an empty room and devastating in an interesting one.

**Bull Rush still needs its rider**, which the momentum version supplies without touching Heave's lane:

> **Bull Rush ◆◆.** Stride, then Shove. Add 3 feet to the push for every 10 feet you moved first.

# SW-103: the Squeeze action

Now genuinely necessary, because a Medium creature is 3 feet wide and any gap under 3 feet is a
squeeze rather than a stroll.

> **Squeeze ◆** *(move)*
> **Requirements** You are entering or moving through a space narrower than your own, but at least
> half as wide.
>
> Move up to **5 feet**, regardless of your Speed. While any part of you is in that space:
>
> - You are **Off-Guard**.
> - **Evade is unavailable** to you. There is nowhere to go.
> - You can attack only with weapons that have the **Close** trait.
>
> A space narrower than half your own stops you entirely. For a Medium creature that means a gap of
> 1½ feet or less.

**Three notes on the shape.**

**Five feet flat, not half Speed.** Half Speed would be 12 feet for a Medium creature, which is a
stroll through a crevice. A flat rate also means a Small character squeezes at the same pace as a big
one, which is right: the constraint is the gap, not your legs.

**Losing Evade is the real cost**, more than the Off-Guard. It takes one of your two Defenses off the
table entirely and leaves you on Guard alone, which is exactly what being wedged in a gap should feel
like, and it reuses a rule you already have from Grabbed.

**The Close clause is where the trait pays off a second time.** A dagger works in a crevice, a pike is
a length of firewood. That is now the third rule pointing at "own a sidearm," alongside Unwieldy and
the reliable Disarm.

# SW-100: Point Out, and the Example of Play

## The Point Out rewrite

> **Point Out ◆** *(auditory, visual)*
> Indicate a creature you have located, whether you can see it or it is merely **Hidden** to you. It
> becomes Hidden rather than Undetected, or Concealed rather than Hidden, to any ally who can **hear or
> see** you.

Two changes. **"Located" rather than Observed**, so you can point at something you have only heard.
And **"hear or see"** rather than both, because the situation in which creatures are Undetected is
darkness, and requiring your allies to see you in the dark is close to requiring the problem not to
exist.

Your own first page is the argument: *"'Left,' she said. 'Low. It's coming along the foundation.'"*
Hrolda has not seen the Seamfinder, she read the mortar dust, and Wren is on an unlit stair. Under the
old wording that exchange is illegal twice over.

## The Example of Play fix

One line breaks under the new spatial rules, and it is Wren's.

> **Wren (Warren):** Last action I Step back out of its reach.

A Step is 3 feet and the Seamfinder reaches ten, so stepping back does nothing at all. Replace it, and
take the opportunity to demonstrate Point Out at the same time:

> **Wren (Warren):** Last action I Stride, right back out of its reach, and I call it for Hrolda.
> Left, low, along the foundation.
>
> > *Point Out ◆ tells your allies where something is. Wren has not seen the Seamfinder clearly since
> > it came out of the wall, but she knows where it is, and that is enough: it stops being Undetected
> > to Hrolda, who can now attack the square it is in. A Stride out of reach would normally provoke,
> > but only from a creature that has Reactive Strike, and this one does not.*

That fixes the distance, shows the corrected Point Out, and quietly teaches that provoking requires
someone who actually has the reaction, which is a question every table will ask in session one.
