## Rulings

252. **Every attack a player makes at several targets rolls once for each target.** Mike's
     decision, wider than the book's "area Attack": a spell, a sweep, a bomb. Each pairing the
     attacker rolls keeps its own die; the Combat window lists one Roll for each defender and the
     card carries a Roll Attack button on each row, for the attacker's owner and the GM. An
     adversary's attack at player characters is unchanged (each player rolls Defense). Adversary
     against adversary is one flat die per defender, the GM's. The Strike or Cast is still paid
     once, at the declaration. Supersedes ruling 155 (one die read against every defender).
253. **A reroll is one pairing's.** The card rerolled is the only one thrown again, and the attack
     card's row follows it. Reverses 0.5.3's "rerolls them all", which followed from the one die.
254. **Each die is its own roll, and one roller's dice are thrown one at a time.** A bonus that
     applies once (a `LIMIT: round` line, a result an ally gave, an armed claim, a custom effect
     lasting one roll) is used by the first die and is gone for the next, which matches Mike's
     draft rule that a Maneuver which rolls twice can use two only when two are available. The
     system opens the next die's dialog only when the die before it has been handed in. Outside
     an encounter a once-a-round bonus has no round to count, so every die gets it, as two
     separate rolls always have. A die is refused as stale only when the answer it was thrown at
     has changed, never because a sibling's die landed first.
255. **Multipliers and Divisors combine where two effects meet on one value, and nowhere else.**
     Mike's decision. Applied to overlapping Difficult terrain, to the party's Travel Speed (an
     Activity's pace and the terrain's), and to the Legs (the Critical Hit effect's halving and
     the first Wound's). Left as the book words them: a second Legs Wound "halves what Speed it
     had left"; a Step, a Crawl, a Leap and a Rush take their own fraction of the Speed you have;
     the damage order keeps its own halving and Critical doubling.
256. **Each overlapping region is one effect, whatever its kind.** Two regions at ×2 cost ×3,
     two of the same kind included. Claude's reading: "one circumstance counted once" would make
     two mud regions ×2, and the book does not say which. An impassable region stays impassable
     and a region of 0 stays free, as when they multiplied.
257. **The Travel Speed is combined for each member first, and its figures round down.** The
     slowest member under the terrain sets the pace, which the terrain can change (Speed 7 at a
     half pace is slower than Speed 4 at full on open ground and faster over Difficult Terrain).
     Feet a minute and miles a day were rounded to the nearest; the book says "Round down the
     final result".
258. **The Critical Hit effects are tracked until a Recenter.** Mike's decision. A Critical Hit
     that is not a Graze and does damage marks the struck Zone; the Arms' −2 Situation is on
     every attack roll (and on an adversary's Attack Threshold and flat roll), the Legs' halved
     Speed is the Speed, the Head's lost Reactions are gone from the Combat window, the Torso's
     is Off-Guard. Recenter ends all four. The Arms' −2 beside an Arms Wound's −2 is −4 (Mike:
     "-4, two effects"); the Legs' beside a Legs Wound is a third (ruling 255).
259. **The Torso's Off-Guard is the Critical Hit's own.** It is put on only when the body is not
     Off-Guard already, and Recenter takes off that one and no other: an Off-Guard the GM set from
     the token palette for another reason stays. A Talent that prevents Off-Guard prevents the
     Torso's effect with it. A Torso Wound's Off-Guard is the Wound's and stays.
260. **Every Critical Hit that does damage sets its Zone's effect, an area Attack's included.**
     The system cannot yet tell an area Attack from another. The switch on each Zone of both
     sheets sets or ends the effect by hand, and that is the GM's remedy for the book's "Area
     Attacks do not automatically gain these location-specific effects". A switch a player
     presses is said in chat, as a Zone Exposed by hand is.
261. **Two Persistent Damage effects of one type are independent.** A Torso Wound's bleed is
     listed beside another bleed, each with its own end-of-round card. "Keep the higher" had been
     read into the code in that one place.
262. **One Reaction at a time is left to the table.** Mike's decision. The attack flow takes one
     answer per attack, the one Trigger the system can identify; elsewhere it cannot tell which
     Trigger a Reaction answers.
263. **A template targets what stands inside it.** Mike's decision. Foundry v14's templates are
     Regions drawn in template mode; when their maker draws, moves or reshapes one, that user's
     targets become the tokens inside, and a button and a macro do it by hand. "Inside" is
     Foundry's own test. A player never targets what they cannot see (a hidden token, or one
     their vision does not reach); the GM's automatic step leaves a hidden token out, since the
     next attack would name it on a public card, and the GM's button takes it. A Region that
     carries a behavior is never a template.
264. **`data/*.xlsx` is the only source for what a Talent, a Maneuver, an Activity or an Endeavor
     says.** Mike's decision in v4.25. The handbook is authoritative for the general rules and
     for the tables it still prints; "when the two disagree, the handbook wins" no longer applies
     to a Talent's or a Maneuver's own text, and no handbook redline is proposed for one.
265. **The redline is written into the edition itself, and the sync reads it as accepted.** Mike's
     decision. The untouched file is in `backup/pre-0.26.0/`. Each change is wording or something
     the system already does (the Subordinate Maneuver rule since 0.25.0, Preparing's two actions
     since 0.20.0). The stamp records the redlined file; when Mike accepts or rejects and saves,
     the build reports the same-numbered file changed once and `node assets/build_all.mjs --check
     --accept-phb` records it again.
266. **Supine is in the redline and not in the system.** Mike's rules are in the Conditions table
     as tracked changes (below). The system has Prone alone, no Roll Over, and reads a rule that
     names Prone as Prone only, until Supine is built. Ruling 213 stands until then.
267. **A Prepared Committed Strike keeps every target.** It remembered one and Finished against
     the first alone; a Prepared Cast already kept them all.
268. **Chapter 5's four capability paragraphs needed nothing.** Nothing in the repository named
     Narrow Point, Armor Pick, Concussive or Cutting.

### Supine

Your rules are sound, and they give Supine a job Prone does not have: Prone can Crawl, Supine can
fight what is standing over it. "Adjacent" is the book's own term (0 feet between spaces) and
Grapples are Attacks in the book, so both of your clauses read as written. The cell, in the
table's one-cell style and in the Prone row's own formatting:

> Lying on your back. Off-Guard; -2 Situation to Attacks, except melee Attacks against adjacent
> creatures (Grapples included); +2 Situation to Evade vs. ranged Attacks. You cannot Move, Step,
> Rush, Leap, or Crawl; forced movement affects you normally. Stand ❶ to clear (you can stand
> directly from your back), or Roll Over ❶ to become Prone.

Three suggestions, each yours to reject:

1. **Roll Over is named in the cell** as the second way out, since the book no longer lists
   Maneuvers and a reader of the Conditions table would not otherwise meet it.
2. **Prone's own −2 is typed Situation**, beside Supine's.
3. **One more sentence, as its own insertion** so it can be rejected alone: "Supine is not Prone:
   a rule that names one does not mean the other." Hold Your Ground's immunity, the final Legs
   Wound ("You fall Prone and cannot Stand") and the Unconscious row all name Prone, and a machine
   has to know whether they mean Supine too. Your own rows (Stand, Kip Up, Drop Prone) name both,
   which reads as two conditions.

Your last bullet was empty, and nothing was added for it. Roll Over mostly matters to a creature
that cannot Stand (the final Legs Wound), since Stand ❶ costs the same and leaves you neither.

Two rows are yours to add in `data/maneuvers.xlsx`, where a Maneuver now lives:

- **Roll Over**: Cost 1, Traits Motion, Requirements "You are Prone or Supine.", Effect "Change
  from Prone to Supine, or from Supine to Prone. You do not leave your space."
- **Drop Prone** already reads "Drop Prone or Supine, your choice."; its `Enabled?` cell is blank.
