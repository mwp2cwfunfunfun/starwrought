# Chapter 4: Equipment

Draft copy. Built on the rules already in Chapter 2: Protection by zone, Load, the Graze rule, and
the standing principle that **equipment never grants a bonus to a roll**.

---

## What gear does, and what it doesn't

> Your sword does not make you accurate and your armor does not make you hard to hit. Skill does
> that, and skill lives in your constellations. **Gear decides what happens when steel finally meets
> you.** A weapon sets the size of the wound; armor sets how much of that wound your body actually
> feels.
>
> This is why there are no magic plusses in STARWROUGHT. Quality, craftsmanship, and enchantment give
> a weapon **traits**, never numbers. A masterwork blade is not a +1 sword; it is a sword that can do
> something an ordinary one cannot.

---

## Armor

> Armor is worn in four places: **Head, Torso, Arms, and Legs.** Each zone holds one piece, and each
> piece is bought, worn, and lost separately. There is no such thing as "a suit of plate" on your
> character sheet; there is a breastplate, a helm, a pair of vambraces, and greaves, and you own as
> many of them as you can afford.

Each piece gives you three things:

- **Protection**, which subtracts from every instance of damage that lands on that zone, a Graze
  included.
- **Load**, the weight and stiffness of it.
- Sometimes a **rider**, one small thing it lets you do or ignore.

**Your torso is the number that matters most**, because ordinary Hits land there. The other three
zones come into play when someone has earned an opening.

### Load, strain, and the check penalty

> Add up the Load of everything you are wearing. That total does two jobs.
>
> **Load strain = your Load minus your Might, minimum 0.** Subtract it from your Evade. Strength does
> not make you stealthy, but it does let you move in steel.
>
> **Your check penalty equals your Load.** It applies to Acrobatics, Athletics, Stealth, and Thievery.
> Might does not help here; bulk is bulk.
>
> Training in **Endure** reduces both by 1, and by 2, 3, or 4 as your rank rises. A Legendary Endure
> character in a full harness moves as though wearing nothing.

> A complete harness of plate is four pieces, Load 4, and about 25 gp. You will not be buying that at
> first level, and that is the point: **everyone starts the game with holes in their armor**, and
> deciding where those holes go is your first real equipment decision.

### Two rules for mixed kit

> **Matched harness.** If all four of your pieces are the same material, add +1 Protection to your
> torso. A complete kit sits and moves better than a collection.
>
> **Clatter.** If two or more of your pieces are mail or plate while your torso piece is neither, your
> kit rattles: you are *noisy* regardless of what the individual pieces say.

### Wearing it

> Putting on or taking off a single piece takes **1 minute**. A full kit takes as long as its pieces.
> You cannot sleep in armor without waking fatigued unless something you are wearing has *comfort*.

## Shields

> A shield is not armor. It grants no Protection, because it does not cover a zone; it is a tool you
> actively interpose, which is why it improves **Guard** and only while you Raise it.

| Shield | Guard bonus when Raised | Hardness | Load | Price |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|---|
| Buckler | +1 | 3 | 0 | 1 gp |
| Shield | +2 | 5 | 1 | 2 gp |
| Tower shield | +2 | 5 | 2 | 10 gp |

> **Raise a Shield ◆** grants the bonus until the start of your next turn.
>
> A **tower shield** can also be used to **Take Cover** while Raised, for +4 Evade instead of the
> usual +2.
>
> **Hardness** matters only if you have the Shield Block talent: it is how much damage the shield
> swallows before the rest reaches you.

## Weapons

> A weapon gives you a **damage die** and a handful of **traits**. It never touches your attack roll;
> that comes from your Weapons proficiency and the attribute your combat style uses.

**Damage on a Hit** is your weapon's dice, plus your Might, plus your weapon specialization (+2 at
Expert, +3 at Master, +4 at Legendary). You roll more dice as you level: two at 4th, three at 12th,
four at 19th.

**Damage on a Graze** is one weapon die and nothing else, as Chapter 2 says.

## Item traits

These extend the Item-related Traits table in Chapter 2.

### Weapon traits

| Trait | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Agile** | Your multiple attack penalty with it is −4 and −8 rather than −5 and −10. |
| **Crushing** | It ignores 2 points of Protection. The mace is the answer to plate. |
| **Deadly dX** | On a critical hit, add one die of the listed size to the damage. |
| **Finesse** | You may use Agility instead of Might on attacks with it. Damage still uses Might. |
| **Nonlethal** | It knocks out rather than kills. |
| **Parry** | Spend ◆ to gain a +1 circumstance bonus to Guard until the start of your next turn. |
| **Range X** | It is meant to be shot, out to X feet, with the usual penalties beyond. |
| **Reach** | It threatens at 10 feet rather than 5. |
| **Reload X** | It takes X Interact actions to ready another shot. |
| **Sweep** | +1 circumstance bonus to attack a target you have not yet attacked this turn. |
| **Thrown X** | You may throw it up to X feet, using your melee attack and Might for damage. |
| **Trip**, **Shove**, **Disarm** | You may attempt that maneuver with this weapon in hand rather than a free hand. |
| **Two-Hand dX** | Wielded in two hands, its damage die becomes dX. |
| **Two-Handed** | It requires both hands. |
| **Versatile X** | You may deal X damage instead of its usual type. |
| **Volley X** | −2 to attacks against targets within X feet. |

### Armor traits

| Trait | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Comfort** | You can sleep in it without waking fatigued. |
| **Noisy** | −1 circumstance penalty to Stealth, on top of your check penalty. |
| **Quiet** | Your check penalty never applies to Stealth while this is your only armor. |

## Starting equipment

> You begin play with **15 gold pieces** and whatever your background plausibly left you holding.
> Spend it on the tables above, plus a travelling kit: pack, bedroll, rope, rations for a week,
> torches, flint, waterskin, and the tools of your trade, all of which cost coppers and none of which
> needs tracking.

> Fifteen gold buys a breastplate and greaves and nothing for your head or arms, or a full mail
> harness with a shield, or a leather kit with a good sword and money left over. **It does not buy a
> complete plate harness.** Where you leave yourself open is a character decision, and your enemies
> will find out.

## Notes for you, not the book

**The economy is deliberately tiny.** Fifteen gold and one price column is enough to make the
piecemeal choice real without committing you to a full equipment economy in a playtest. If it drags,
replace prices with a flat "**Load 4 of armor and one weapon set**" allowance and delete the money.

**Crushing is the whole anti-armor rule**, delivered as a weapon trait the way you wanted. It sits on
fists, clubs, maces, warhammers, mauls, staves, and slings, which is historically correct and gives
blunt weapons a reason to exist beside swords. Note it makes the **Brawling** style quietly excellent
against armored foes, since fists are crushing.

**Two things to check against your own rules.** Chapter 2's Strike entry says *"your combat style sets
which attribute you add,"* while *finesse* says the weapon lets you choose Agility. Those can
disagree; you need to say which wins. And **Parry** overlaps Raise a Shield, since both spend an
action for a circumstance bonus to Guard. That is fine as long as they never stack, which the
circumstance rule already handles, but it is worth a sentence.

**Gauntlets are a weapon as well as armor**, which is why the arms piece mentions it. If that feels
like clutter for a playtest, cut the clause and let Brawling cover unarmed work.

**Not included, on purpose:** magic items, alchemical gear, consumables, and encumbrance beyond Load.
None of them are needed to test whether the Protection and zone system is fun.
