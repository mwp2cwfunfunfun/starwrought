## 1. What was built

A complete Foundry game system, not a module on top of another one, as `starwrought-core-design.md`
§10 called for. Actor and Item data models, three sheets, a check engine, a damage pipeline,
conditions as token statuses, initiative, and seven compendium packs holding all 170 Talents,
30 Constellations, 29 weapons, 20 armor pieces, 3 shields, 46 Actions, 13 chassis Items, 20 pages
of reference tables, and 4 macros.

The design brief was followed literally: **arithmetic and tracked state are free, decisions cost.**
The system computes Attributes from Talents, rank from spend and level, Hit Points from Ancestry
and Calling, Protection per Zone with the material step, the damage order of operations, Load
Strain with the Endure relief, Dying and Wounded, and the Multiple Attack Penalty ladder. What it
asks the player is what the rules make a decision: which Defense answers the blow, which Zone the
graze opened, which Constellation the critical belonged to.

Verified in a live world, not on paper. A level 1 Human Berserker of Serrovane was built to the
Chapter 3 budget and every derived number checked by hand: Might 7 points to +2, Guard threshold
17, torso Protection 3 dropping to 2 against piercing because mail turns it poorly, Hit Points
22 = 10 + (8 + 4). Then a Strike, a Graze, a Critical, a drop to 0, a Recovery, a Refusal of
Death, and a Flare, all through the chat cards.

## 2. Rulings made on ambiguous text

These are the places two readings were defensible. Each says which one shipped and why.

**2.1 The Origin's rank is pooled. Handbook wins over the data.**
`trees.json` authors Human and Serrovane as separate skies with separate point totals; the PHB
says "your Ancestry, Bloodline and Culture merge into it" and v0.29 says "Origin progression is
merged: rank counts all points across its sources." The system pools every `origin`-category
Constellation into one rank and one Proficiency Bonus, while still showing each sky's own spend.
The sheet says "3 pts pooled" on the group header so nobody has to guess.
**Confirmed by Mike, 2026-08-27: correct, the Origin is pooled.** No change.

**2.2 Deadly dX lands after the critical doubling.**
The order of operations doubles at step 5. "A Critical Hit deals double damage. Roll all dice and
add all damage modifiers, then double it" is followed by "Apply additional Critical Effects of your
weapon," which is where Deadly lives. So the extra die is added after doubling and is not itself
doubled. **The other reading is real**: Deadly could be one of the dice rolled at step 2, doubled
with everything else, which roughly doubles what the trait is worth. pf2e precedent agrees with the
shipped reading.
**Ruled by Mike, 2026-08-27: correct, Deadly lands after the doubling.** The code already did
this; the handbook now says so, in the Deadly dX trait entry and in the order-of-operations note.

**2.3 A Defense Threshold is 10 plus the check modifier.**
The handbook never states the conversion between a Defense check and a Defense Threshold. The
Example of Play is consistent with `10 + mod`: Kessa's Evade is d20+6 and the Seamfinder's Evade
Threshold is 16 at the same level. The system uses `10 + level + Attribute + Proficiency`, matching
the printed ability-DC formula.
**Ruled by Mike, 2026-08-27: correct, it is ten plus the modifier.** Now stated explicitly in the
handbook, in Roll Playing Conventions where monsters-carry-Thresholds is introduced, and again in
the Key Terms entry for Threshold.

**2.4 The Graze opens the Zone that was struck, and the defender may move it.**
The rules say the defender chooses the Zone on a Graze. Software cannot stop mid-roll and ask, so
the damage card carries a Zone selector (defaulting to the Torso) and the Zone chosen there is both
where the damage lands and what becomes Exposed. Moving it afterwards is one click on the sheet.
**Ruled by Mike, 2026-08-27: accepted, provided Protection still reduces the damage when the Zone
was not already Exposed.** It does, and there is now a test for it: a Graze of 5 on a closed Arms
with Protection 2 deals 3 and then opens the Zone; a second Graze of 5 on the now Exposed Arms
deals all 5.

**2.5 Load Strain relief is attached to owning Endure Training, not to Endure rank alone.**
The relief clause is written on the root Talent, so the system applies it only when the character
owns at least one point in Endure. Every character starts Trained in Endure, so this is invisible
at the table.
**Answered by Mike, 2026-08-27: Endure Training is reworded in PHB v3.2.** The relief now starts
at Expert rather than Trained: 1 at Expert, 2 at Master, 3 at Legendary. `data/defenses.xlsx` and
`LOAD_RELIEF` both follow it, so a first-level character in mail now carries one more point of
Strain than they did an hour ago.

**2.6 Weapon Handling steps the rank, and at level 1 that cliff is four points.**
Practiced without Familiarity means "Weapons Proficiency Bonus one rank lower", which at Trained is
Untrained: +4 becomes +0. Technical without Familiarity is Untrained outright. That is what the
table says, and it is what shipped.
**Ruled by Mike, 2026-08-27: intended.** No change. Worth watching at the table anyway: a Practiced
longsword in the hands of a first-level character who did not spend a point on Weapon Familiarity
is a 20% swing.

**2.7 The Multiple Attack Penalty is chosen, not counted.**
The roll dialog asks which attack this is. The system does not track attacks per turn, because
readied actions, reactions, and Reactive Strike all break a naive counter, and a wrong automatic
answer is worse than an asked one. The ladder itself is automatic, including Agile's −4/−8.
**Confirmed by Mike, 2026-08-27, and improved since.** The dialog now *opens* on the step the
character has actually reached, counted from the start of its turn, and the player can still move
it. That keeps the arithmetic free without letting a counter be wrong in silence: a reaction
neither suffers the penalty nor accrues it, and no counter can know that. The count lives in
memory on the rolling client, resets when your turn comes round, and there is a world setting to
turn it off.

**2.8 An action's cost is read from the glyphs in its name, and it needed a real field.**
**Mike asked for one, 2026-08-27, and he was right: the old parser was broken.** It counted every
diamond in the string, so "Strike ◆ to ◆◆◆" came out as a flat three-action activity, and so did
Grapple, Shove, Trip, Disarm, Reposition and Ready. Actions and Talents now carry `cost`, `costMax`
and `costMode`, the parser reads both ends of the range, and the sheets print what the handbook
prints: "◆ to ◆◆◆" for Strike, "◆ or ◆◆◆" for Disarm, which is one or three with nothing between.
Ten actions were wrong and are now right.

The parser and the slug rule both moved into `module/config.mjs` and the content build imports
them, so the two copies that could disagree are now one.

## 3. One thing that looked like a data bug, and is not

**Armored Fighting's mixed per-Talent feeds are deliberate. Ruled by Mike, 2026-08-27.**

The `Armored Fighting Training` root carries a per-Talent `Feeds = Might` override in
`combat_styles.xlsx` while the Constellation's own Key Attribute is Presence, so buying the root
hands you a Might point but your Armored Fighting checks still use Presence. It was raised here as
a suspected leftover. It is not: **the mixed feeds are the design, and the Root Talent uses Might.**

The whole Constellation splits four ways, which is exactly the v0.10 per-Talent feed rule doing its
job. Recorded here so nobody later "fixes" it into uniformity:

| Feeds | Talents |
|---|---|
| Might | Armored Fighting Training (root), Ironclad, Trust the Harness, Lock Down, Punishing Shove, Shoulder Check |
| Presence (the Constellation's Key Attribute) | Flowing Defense, Supple Harness, Roll With It |
| Wits | Turn the Scales, Layered Guard |
| Agility | Loose Guard, Light on Your Feet |

Verified in the live world: four Talents bought across four feeds put one Attribute Point into each
of Might, Presence, Wits and Agility, while Armored Fighting's rank counted all four and its checks
stayed on Presence. That is the rule working, not drifting.

*Still open, and untouched by this ruling:* the Constellation-level contradiction `CLAUDE.md`
records, where the PHB's section heading says *Combat Style • Presence* and the Combat Styles
summary table says **Might**. The data follows the heading and so does this system.

## 4. What the system does not do

Stated plainly rather than left to be discovered:

- **Prerequisites are recorded but not enforced.** A Talent's `Requires` list is stored, shown on
  the Item sheet, and printed on the card, but nothing stops a player buying a Talent out of order
  or above their tier gate. The rank gates *are* computed correctly; they simply do not block.
- **Talent Point types are reference, not budget.** Origin, Calling, Defense, Skill, Lore, Opening,
  Comet, Milestone and Deferred points are documented in the Rules compendium. The system does not
  track how many of each a character is owed or has spent, and does not enforce the no-banking rule.
- **There is no character-creation wizard.** `Starwrought_App.html` has one and it is good. Here
  you drag Talents from the compendium onto a sheet, and the Constellation opens itself when you do.
- **Cover, Concealment and detection states are tables, not automation.** They need a judgement
  about a line between two tokens, which is the standing risk `CLAUDE.md` names.
- **The Root Rule is not enforced here.** `xlsx_to_trees.py` warns on it, which is the right place;
  the two deliberate Human bloodline violators ship as authored.
- **No magic**, because the playtest has none.

## 6. Bugs found and fixed during the build

Recorded because `CLAUDE.md`'s "look at it" rule earned its keep again. None of these would have
been caught by a test suite that did not launch the application.

| Symptom on screen | Cause |
|---|---|
| The system loaded but no sheets registered | `CONFIG.STARWROUGHT` is an ES module namespace, and `init` was assigning a property to it |
| Every Actor failed data preparation | `Actor#getRollData` returns the live system object; the override read a derived value that does not exist yet when Active Effects ask for it, and also wrote onto the character |
| A Flare would light but never go out | An update merges objects, so removing a key needs the `-=` deletion syntax |
| The adversary sheet printed `STARWROUGHT.ZONE.HEAD` | A spread after the localized label overwrote it with the raw key |
| Apply buttons broke the word "Apply" in half | The chat sidebar is narrower than the row assumed |
| Familiarity and the damage-modifier fields looked empty | `system.familiarity` is a Set, and the join helper only knew about Arrays |

## 7. The second pass, after the rulings came back

Everything in section 2 is answered. What actually moved:

**The handbook.** `Starwrought_Players_Handbook_v3.3.docx` is written from Mike's v3.2 by
`assets/phb_edit.py`, a new surgical editor that appends a sentence to a named paragraph and copies
every other byte of the package through untouched. Five text changes, verified three ways: a diff of
the two documents (22 zip entries, only `word/document.xml` differs, 3095 paragraphs before and
after, exactly the five intended lines changed), an XML well-formedness check on all 19 XML parts,
and an independent read with `python-docx`, which opens it as 800 paragraphs, 95 tables and 7
sections with the new sentences in place, including the one inside the Deadly trait's table cell.

The file is about 16 KB smaller than v3.2 despite gaining text. That is only recompression: Python
rewrote the zip with its own deflate settings. Every entry is present and byte-identical apart from
the document itself.

| Where | What it now says |
|---|---|
| Weapon Traits, *Deadly dX* | It is a critical effect of the weapon rather than one of the dice rolled for damage, so it is added after the damage has been doubled, and is not itself doubled. |
| Damage order of operations | A weapon's Deadly die lands at the same seam: after the doubling in step 5 and before Protection in step 6. |
| Roll Playing Conventions | A Threshold is always ten plus the modifier of the check it stands in for: 10 + level + Attribute Bonus + Proficiency Bonus, and any bonuses and penalties that apply. |
| Key Terms, *Threshold* | A Guard of +7 is a Guard Threshold of 17. |
| Cover page | PLAYTEST EDITION v3.3 |

The version was bumped rather than v3.2 being edited in place, so the file Mike authored as v3.2 is
still exactly that. **If he would rather these edits simply be part of v3.2, rename the file and
change the edition run back; nothing else depends on the number.**

**The data.** `data/defenses.xlsx` carries the v3.2 wording of Endure Training, so the spreadsheet,
the app, the Constellation Compendium and the Foundry packs all say the same thing. The whole
pipeline was re-run: converter (zero errors, the two known Human bloodline warnings), injector,
plates, character sheets, compendium, Foundry content, style guard clean.

**The code.**

- `LOAD_RELIEF` starts at Expert: 0 / 0 / 1 / 2 / 3. A first-level character in mail and an open
  helm now carries Load Strain 3 rather than 2, and Evade +2 rather than +3.
- Actions and Talents carry `cost`, `costMax` and `costMode`, and `parseActionCost` reads both ends
  of a range instead of counting diamonds. Ten actions were silently wrong.
- `actionName` strips the connective as well as the glyphs, so the action formerly called
  "Disarm  or" is called Disarm.
- The Multiple Attack Penalty dialog opens on the step the character has reached this turn, counted
  in memory on the rolling client and reset when their turn comes round. World setting
  `trackMap` turns it off.
- `slugify` and `parseActionCost` live once, in `module/config.mjs`, and the content build imports
  them from there. The module is now documented as having to stay free of Foundry globals at module
  scope for that reason.
