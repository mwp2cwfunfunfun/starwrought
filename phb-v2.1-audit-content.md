# SW-064: the bracing question, because it changes SW-003

> *"But if you are bracing high, doesn't that mean you are guarding high, leaving your lower body
> exposed?"*

You have found the real fork. There are two physically true stories and they point in opposite
directions.

**Story A: the zone you guarded WITH opens.** Your shield arm meets the blow and is driven out of
line, so your Arms open. What failed is the thing that did the work.

**Story B: the zone you did NOT guard opens.** You braced high, your guard is committed up there, so
your legs are open. What failed is the place you left.

Both are real fencing. But they produce very different games, and B has a hole in it.

**Under B, the defender always wins the exchange.** You brace with your best-armored zone, and the
zone that opens is the one you left uncovered, which is the one you already did not care about. The
cost lands exactly where it hurts least, every single time, by design rather than by accident.

**Under A, the cost is real.** Guard with the shield and your Arms open, which is where your shield
and probably your vambraces are. The zone you are most invested in is the zone that gets driven out
of line. That is what makes the Graze cost bite, and it is what makes vambraces worth buying to a
shield fighter and greaves worth buying to a great-weapon fighter. It gives all four zones a build
reason to exist.

There is also a narrative reading of A that answers your instinct directly: bracing high does not
leave your legs open, because you did not *stop* covering your legs. You committed your guard to
one line and the blow found that line and beat it. The opening is in your guard, not in your
posture.

**My recommendation is A**, and the sentence I would print is: *"Your guard is what failed, so your
guard is what opens."* If you already ruled B, the three talents below still work, but expect the
Graze cost to feel free within about two sessions.

turning Grazes into Hits is powerful.

# SW-010: the three talents that assumed the old zone rule

### Sixth Sense, rebuilt

The old version had nothing to redirect. This version instead solves a problem the playtest already
had: **Awareness has no combat job.** It is the only Defense that never answers a blow, so a
Wits character's best Defense is dead weight in a fight.

> **Sixth Sense** · T · Requires *Awareness Training*
> Once per encounter, after you roll a Defense but before the outcome is announced, you may add your
> Wits to that roll.
> *Key Attribute: Wits*

That is Awareness lending its attribute to Evade or Guard once a fight, which is exactly what "you
saw it coming" should buy, and it makes Wits worth having on a fighter.

### Kneel Behind the Shield, reworded

Names the Zone directly, so it no longer depends on the general rule.

> **Kneel Behind the Shield ↺** · T · Requires *Guard Training* · *(posture)*
> You must have a shield Raised. +4 to your Guard against every attack until the start of your next
> turn. Your **Arms** become Exposed, and your Speed is 0 until you spend an action to rise.
> *Key Attribute: Presence*

### Clinch, tightened

This one was already rule-agnostic; it just needed to say so cleanly.

> **Clinch ◆** *(stance)* · Brawling
> While you are in this stance, when an enemy Grazes one of your attacks, **you** choose which of its
> Zones opens rather than the enemy. At Master rank, a creature you have Grabbed cannot Recenter.

# SW-026: Guile, and a Diplomacy Constellation

## Keep Guile. Do not rename it to Misdirection.

**Misdirection is narrower than the job.** Guile currently covers **Feint** and **Lie**, and
Awareness is explicitly "the check against Sneak, Feint, and Lie." Misdirection covers the feint
beautifully and does not cover lying at all, so you would need a third skill to hold what fell out.

**Guile also contrasts correctly with Diplomacy.** Diplomacy wins people over straight; Guile works
them crooked. That is a clean pair a player understands instantly, and it does not need a third
term. Misdirection against Diplomacy is not a pair, it is two unrelated things.

**One thing to settle while you are here:** if Diplomacy is Presence, make **Guile Wits**. That
gives each its own attribute and its own fantasy: Diplomacy is force of personality, Guile is speed
of thought. It also resolves SW-019 in the direction that creates the most build variety, since
otherwise both social skills feed the same attribute and a face character has no choice to make.

# SW-029 and SW-046: Zone critical effects

The pattern is consistent so nobody has to memorize eight things: **the Exposed version always
includes the ordinary version, plus one escalation.** Everything here uses conditions already in
*Conditions in Brief*, except the Head's ordinary effect, which is written out plainly.

| Zone | Critical Hit | Critical Hit on an **Exposed** Zone |
|---|---|---|
| **Head** | The target cannot use reactions until it Recenters. | As above, and it is **Stunned 1**. |
| **Torso** | The target is **Off-Guard** until it Recenters. | As above, and it is **Slowed 1** until it Recenters. |
| **Arms** | The target takes a −2 circumstance penalty to attack rolls until it Recenters. | As above, and it drops one held item of the attacker's choice. |
| **Legs** | The target's Speed is halved until it Recenters. | As above, and it is knocked **prone**. |

*Why these.* The Head loses your reaction, which in a game this reaction-heavy is a real loss
without being a lost turn, and escalates to Stunned, which is. The Torso is the wind going out of
you: Off-Guard now, an action gone if your armor was already displaced. The Arms escalate to the
dropped shield or weapon, which is the single most tactically interesting outcome in the table and
the reason SW-046 was worth filling in properly. The Legs are the one you already had right.

**Two things this fixes beyond the placeholder.** It gives the **Head** the routine relevance it has
been missing, so helmets stop being the worst purchase in the game. And it puts real teeth behind
the Graze economy, which per SW-041 is now the thing keeping "expose your least-armored Zone" from
being a free move.

# SW-030: precision damage, for the Damage Rolls section

> **Precision damage** is extra damage that comes from *where* you put the blow rather than how hard
> you swing it. *Sneak Attack*, *Mark Prey*, and *Finisher* all deal it.
>
> - Precision damage is added to a **Hit** and to a **Critical Hit**, and is doubled along with
>   everything else on a Critical Hit.
> - Precision damage is **never** added to a Graze. A Graze is one weapon die and nothing else.
> - Precision damage is part of the same instance of damage as the Strike that carried it. The
>   Zone's Protection is subtracted once from the total, not once from each part.
> - A creature with no anatomy to find takes no precision damage at all. An ooze has no throat.

# SW-031: Temporary Hit Points

## For Key Terms

> **Temporary Hit Points.** A pool of Hit Points laid over your own and spent first. They are not
> healing, they do not stack with another pool, and they vanish when the effect that granted them
> ends.

## For Character Mechanics

> **Temporary Hit Points** are a buffer laid over your Hit Points. Damage comes out of them first,
> and only what is left over reaches your real Hit Points.
>
> - **They are not healing.** Anything that restores Hit Points restores your own, never these.
> - **You can only have one pool at a time.** If an effect would give you a second, keep whichever
>   pool is larger and discard the other. They never add together.
> - **They last** until they are spent or until the effect that granted them ends, whichever comes
>   first.
> - **Losing your last Temporary Hit Point is not the same as being reduced to 0 Hit Points.** It
>   does not make you dying and it does not make you Wounded.
> - **Protection applies first.** Protection reduces the damage itself, so it comes off before
>   anything reaches your Temporary Hit Points.

That last bullet is the one that will actually come up at the table, given how Rage and the Zone
system interact.

# SW-038: the specifics you asked for

The Evade and Guard availability limits are printed three times and **no two of them agree**.

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| p421, Ch. 2 | "You cannot slip a blow while you are **grabbed, restrained, or immobilized**, and you cannot meet one you never saw coming, or with nothing in your hands and no hand free. Those limits apply to attacks only. Against poison, fear, blasts, and **everything that is not aimed at you by a creature, all four Defenses are always available** (where they make sense)." |
| p745, Evade Training | "Against physical attacks it is unavailable while you are grabbed, restrained, or immobilized; **against everything else it is always available**." |
| p748, Guard Training | "Against physical attacks it is unavailable while you are unaware of the attack or have nothing in hand and no hand free; **against mental effects it is always available**." |

**Three concrete discrepancies:**

1. **"Immobilized" is not a condition in this game.** It appears in p421 and p745 and is absent from
   *Conditions in Brief*, which has Grabbed and Restrained but nothing called Immobilized. *(This is
   a new finding; call it **SW-075**.)*
2. **Guard's carve-out is narrower than the general rule.** p421 says all four Defenses are
   available against anything not aimed at you by a creature, which includes a blast or a falling
   ceiling. p748 says Guard is always available "against mental effects," which by omission suggests
   you cannot Guard a blast. A player will ask this in session one.
3. **Only p421 carries "(where they make sense)."** The other two state their rule absolutely, so
   whichever a player reads first is the one they will believe.

**The fix: state it once, in Ch. 2, and make both Training entries point at it.**

> **When a Defense is unavailable.** These limits apply to **attacks only**, meaning something a
> creature aimed at you.
>
> - **Evade** is unavailable while you are Grabbed or Restrained. You cannot slip what is already
>   holding you.
> - **Guard** is unavailable while you are unaware of the attack, or while you have nothing in hand
>   and no hand free. You cannot meet what you did not see or cannot reach.
>
> Against everything else, including blasts, traps, collapsing floors, poison, and fear, **all four
> Defenses are available whenever the GM judges them relevant.**

Then both Training entries end with: *"See When a Defense is Unavailable, Chapter 2."*

# SW-055: additional armor, including scale

## The gap first

Matched harness is currently unreachable below Protection 3, because the **Head** zone has no
Protection 2 option and the **Arms** and **Legs** have no Protection 1 option. So the bonus for a kit
that sits well is available only to people in mail or plate, which is backwards. Three cheap pieces
close it.

| Piece | Zone | Protection | Load | Price | Traits |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|---|---|
| **Padded sleeves** | Arms | 1 | 0 | 1 gp | Padded, Comfort |
| **Padded leggings** | Legs | 1 | 0 | 1 gp | Padded, Comfort |
| **Leather cap** | Head | 2 | 0 | 2 gp | Leather, Quiet |

Now matched harness is achievable at every tier: **Protection 1 for 4 gp at Load 0**, Protection 2
for 9 gp at Load 0, Protection 3 for 25 gp, Protection 4 for 45 gp. A starting character can afford
the bonus, which is the point.

**One wording fix that falls out of this.** Matched harness says "reduce your Load by 1," and the
two light sets are already Load 0, so the rule currently produces Load −1. Add "to a minimum of 0."

## Scale

Scale needs a reason to exist that is not "mail but slightly different." The interesting slot is
**the material with no weakness**, because the whole armor-of-proof system is rock-paper-scissors and
the option to step out of the game is worth paying for.

| Piece | Zone | Protection | Load | Price | Traits |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|---|---|
| **Scale coif** | Head | 3 | 1 | 10 gp | Scale, Noisy |
| **Scale hauberk** | Torso | 3 | 1 | 20 gp | Scale, Noisy |
| **Scale sleeves** | Arms | 3 | 1 | 10 gp | Scale, Noisy |
| **Scale skirt** | Legs | 3 | 1 | 10 gp | Scale, Noisy |

> **Scale turns nothing poorly.** Overlapping plates on a backing catch the cut, blunt the thrust,
> and spread the blow. Scale has no material weakness, which is why it costs roughly twice what mail
> does for the same Protection.

**The trade is gold, not Load**, which I chose deliberately: pricing it in Load would have made a
full scale harness unwearable given how Load Strain compounds, and pricing it in Protection would
have made it strictly worse than mail. As gold, it reads as the aspirational purchase, the thing you
save for, and it never obsoletes mail for a starting character.

**If you would rather the trade be Load**, the alternative is Protection 3, **Load 2**, priced at
mail rates. That version is for characters with high Might who can eat the Strain, which is a
different and also good design. I would not do both.

# SW-058: the multiply and divide convention

Replace the current sentence with this.

> **Multipliers and divisors combine by addition, not by multiplication.** Rather than applying each
> one in turn, count how far past 1 each takes you, add those together, and apply the result once.
>
> | You have | Result | Not |
> |---|---|---|
> | Two effects that double | ×3 | ×4 |
> | Two effects that triple | ×5 | ×9 |
> | A double and a triple | ×4 | ×6 |
> | Two effects that halve | ÷3 | ÷4 |
> | Three effects that halve | ÷4 | ÷8 |
>
> The rule exists so that stacked multipliers cannot run away from the table. Two doublings make a
> blow three times as bad, which is plenty.
>
> **Round down once, at the end.** Carry fractions through the calculation and drop them only when
> you have a final number.

That last line is a decision, not just wording: your current text says "Fractional numbers must
always be rounded down" without saying *when*, and rounding at every step gives measurably different
results from rounding once. Once at the end is both kinder to the player and less arithmetic.
