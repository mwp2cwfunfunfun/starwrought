# Awareness, Light, Cover, and Concealment

Drop-in rules. Two notes before the text.

**The term is Awareness, not Perception.** Perception is the D&D and Pathfinder word; your game already
spent it on Wits and built a Defense called Awareness. Using both would leave a reader hunting for a
Perception statistic that does not exist. Everything below says Awareness.

**No creature in STARWROUGHT sees in the dark.** That was your ruling when you cut darkvision from the
ancestries, and it is the single most important fact about this section, because it means light is a
resource that every party spends and no build gets to skip. These rules are written to make that pay
off rather than merely to be consistent with it.

---

# Awareness

> **Awareness** is how much of the world you are actually taking in, and it works two ways, as every
> Defense does. **Your Awareness check** is what you roll to find something. **Your Awareness
> Threshold** is the number someone else has to beat to Sneak, Hide, Feint, or Lie to you.
>
> Everything about being hard to find runs through three states, and Awareness is how you climb down
> them.
>
> | State | What your enemy knows | Effect |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Concealed** | Exactly where you are, but not clearly | You gain a +2 circumstance bonus to your Defense |
> | **Hidden** | Roughly where you are | +4 circumstance bonus, and attacks against you must guess your square |
> | **Undetected** | Nothing | You cannot be targeted at all. An enemy may attack the square it last believed you occupied |
>
> **Seek ◆** *(concentrate)* moves one creature one rung back toward you: Undetected becomes Hidden,
> Hidden becomes Concealed, Concealed becomes plainly seen. A critical success skips a rung.
>
> **A creature that cannot see you is off-guard to you.** That is what being unseen buys on offense,
> and it is why the Ambusher wants the dark.
>
> **Sight is the only sense that finds anything precisely.** Hearing, smell, and a hand on the floor
> can tell you roughly where something is, which is enough to make it **Hidden**, and never enough to
> make it plainly seen. In darkness, Seek is a matter of listening, so it can bring a creature to
> Hidden and no further.

# Light

> **Nothing sees in the dark.** No ancestry, no bloodline, no talent in this playtest grants sight
> without light. Torches are not flavour text; they are the reason your party can fight at all.
>
> | Light | Effect on what you are looking at |
> |---|---|
> | **Bright light** | Nothing. You see normally |
> | **Dim light** | Creatures and objects are **Concealed** to you |
> | **Darkness** | Creatures and objects are **Undetected** to you |
>
> **Light works both ways, and this is the rule that matters.** A creature holding or standing in a
> light source is in bright light, so it is never Concealed and never Hidden, and anything out in the
> dark can see it perfectly while it can see nothing at all. Whoever carries the torch is the one
> everybody can find.
>
> | Light source | Bright light | Dim light beyond that | Notes |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Candle** | 5 ft | 5 ft | Goes out in wind |
> | **Torch** | 20 ft | 20 ft | Burns an hour. Also an improvised weapon: 1d4 bludgeoning plus 1 fire |
> | **Lantern** | 30 ft | 30 ft | ◆ to shutter or unshutter |
> | **Bullseye lantern** | 60 ft in a cone | 20 ft around you | ◆ to turn it somewhere new |
> | **Campfire** | 20 ft | 20 ft | |
>
> **Blinded.** Everything is Undetected to you, and you automatically fail any check that needs sight.
> **Deafened.** Auditory effects fail on you, and you cannot Seek by sound.

**Why dim light is Concealed rather than a penalty.** It reuses a state the book already has, so there
is nothing new to learn, and it means the entire lighting system is two rows of a table rather than a
subsystem. It also makes a torch mechanically worth carrying: stepping into bright light strips a +2
off everything you want to hit.

# Cover

**How to tell the three grades apart: count the Zones.** You already have four parts of a body, so
you already have the ruler. Cover is not a judgement about how much of you is behind the wall, it is a
count of which Zones the wall is in front of.

> **Cover is something solid between you and the attack.** Adjudicate it in four steps.
>
> **1. Draw the line.** From the centre of the attacker's space to the centre of yours. If nothing
> solid is in the way, you have no cover.
>
> **2. Name the Zones the obstacle is in front of**, from the attacker's side.
>
> **3. Count them.**
>
> | Zones covered | Grade | Effect |
> |:-:|---|---|
> | **1 or 2** | **Cover** | +2 circumstance bonus to your Defense |
> | **3** | **Greater cover** | +4 circumstance bonus to your Defense |
> | **4** | **Total cover** | You cannot be targeted at all |
>
> **4. Apply the Zone rule.** **An attacker cannot place a blow on a covered Zone.** If a Hit would
> land on one, it lands on your **Arms** instead, or on your **Head** if the Arms are covered too. A
> **Critical Hit** ignores this and chooses freely, because finding the gap is what a critical hit is.

## Worked examples

| You are behind | Zones covered | Count | Grade |
|---|---|:-:|---|
| A low wall, a cart, a table | Legs | 1 | **Cover** |
| A battlement, a windowsill, a barrel | Legs, Torso | 2 | **Cover** |
| A corner or doorframe, edged around | Legs, Torso, Arms | 3 | **Greater cover** |
| An arrow slit, with only your eyes clear | Legs, Torso, Arms | 3 | **Greater cover** |
| Anything you are entirely behind | all four | 4 | **Total cover** |

## The rulings that follow from it

> **Cover is judged per attacker, every time.** The same wall gives you Greater cover against the
> archer in front of you and nothing at all against the one who has walked around your flank. Nothing
> about cover is a property of you; it is a property of the line between you.
>
> **Total cover works both ways.** If nothing can reach you, you cannot see out either, so you cannot
> target them any more than they can target you.
>
> **A creature never gives more than Cover**, whatever it happens to be standing in front of. Bodies
> are not walls. This is also why an ally between you and an archer is worth +2 and not +4, however
> large the ally.
>
> **Take Cover ◆** raises your cover one grade: no cover becomes Cover, Cover becomes Greater cover.
> It lasts until you move or attack. A **tower shield** lets you Take Cover with nothing to get behind.
>
> **Cover helps whichever Defense you roll.** A pillar between you does not care whether you were
> slipping the blow or meeting it.

## Two things this gets for free

**Going prone behind a low wall upgrades your own cover, and no rule is needed to say so.** Standing,
the wall is in front of your Legs, which is one Zone and Cover. Lying down behind it, the same wall is
in front of your Legs, Torso and Arms, which is three Zones and Greater cover. The count does the work.

**The tension is the point.** Duck behind a battlement and nothing can touch your torso, which is where
ordinary Hits go, so you have made yourself very hard to hurt. But the only things left to hit are your
arms and your head, and a Critical Hit to the head is the worst outcome in the game. Cover stops being
a flat +2 and becomes a decision about which part of you you are willing to risk.

# Concealment

> **Concealment is not being clearly seen**, where cover is having something solid in the way. It makes
> you **Concealed**: a +2 circumstance bonus to your Defense, while your enemy still knows exactly
> where you are.
>
> Dim light, fog, smoke, heavy rain, thick snow, and a blur all conceal. None of them hide you, which
> is why **Hide ◆** and **Sneak ◆** exist: they are how you turn concealment into being genuinely
> unfound, and both are Stealth against the Awareness of whoever might spot you.

---

# What stacks, and what does not

> Cover, concealment, a raised shield, and Take Cover are all **circumstance** bonuses, so **only the
> largest of them applies.** A shield behind a barrel is not +4; it is +2, whichever way you got there.
>
> This is the rule that keeps the section from collapsing. Without it, a concealed character with a
> raised shield behind greater cover would be at +8 and simply could not be hit.

# Quick reference

| | |
|---|---|
| Dim light | Concealed, +2 |
| Darkness | Undetected, cannot be targeted |
| Cover, 1 or 2 Zones covered | +2, and covered Zones cannot be chosen |
| Greater cover, 3 Zones covered | +4, same |
| Total cover, all 4 Zones covered | Cannot be targeted, and you cannot target out |
| Concealed | +2 |
| Hidden | +4, and your foe must guess a 5-foot area to attack you |
| Undetected | Cannot be targeted |
| Carrying a light | You are never Concealed or Hidden |
| All of the above | Circumstance bonuses. Only the largest applies |

# Three things this section fixes

**Blinded and Deafened were referenced and never defined.** The *Visual* trait says an effect "fails on
the blinded" and the *Auditory* trait says it "fails on the deafened," but neither condition appears in
*Conditions in Brief*. Both are defined above and should be added to that table.

**Take Cover currently gives its bonus to Evade only**, which means a Guard-focused character gains
nothing from standing behind a pillar. Changed above to whichever Defense you roll.

**The tower shield footnote can be simplified.** It currently reads "+4 Evade instead of Take Cover's
usual +2 Evade," which double-states the bonus. Under these rules a tower shield simply lets you Take
Cover with nothing to get behind, and Take Cover already means Greater cover.
