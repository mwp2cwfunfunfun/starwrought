# The Seamfinder

*Opening fiction, Player's Handbook. Draft v1.*

---

"It's behind the mortar line!" Wren shouted from the top of the stair, and then there was no more
time for warning.

The thing came out of the wall's shadow without a sound, and it did not go for the plate. It went
for the gap between Hrolda's pauldron and her cuirass, the finger's width of leather and buckle
that no armorer has ever solved. Hrolda got her shield around in time to catch most of it. Most
was not all. The point skidded off the rim, opened the strap, and left a cold line across her
shoulder before she could set her feet again.

She kept her ground. That was the choice, and it cost her: her guard was driven wide, and she felt
the whole line of her defense go loose, the way a door hangs wrong once the frame has shifted.

"Third night," Hrolda said through her teeth. "Third night and it still hasn't touched a single
plate of me."

"Because it isn't trying to." Wren dropped the last four steps without touching them, landing soft
as a dropped cloak. "It goes for the seams. That's the whole of it. Every guard it's killed in this
quarter died of a scratch."

They had come to Coldharrow for a bounty they had both privately decided was a story. Something in
the old stonecutters' quarter had been killing watchmen for a season, always at night, always in
armor, always through a gap. The city called it the Seamfinder. Hrolda had spent thirty years
learning to be the least killable thing in any room, and she was beginning to take it personally.

She rolled the shoulder, tested it, breathed. The wound was shallow. The problem was not the wound.
The problem was that her guard was still out of line, and she knew better than to meet a second
pass like that.

She took the moment and set herself: feet squared, shield back on the centerline, everything
gathered in. It cost her the chance to strike. It bought her the ability to survive being struck.

The dust told them first. A fine grey sift came out of the joints of the wall beside them, the way
old mortar does when something leans its weight on stone that has not been touched in a century.
Hrolda had grown up reading walls. She did not have to decide to notice it; her blood noticed for
her, the way it always had.

"Left," she said. "Low. It's coming along the foundation."

Wren was already moving, and moving was the whole of Wren's art. When the Seamfinder came out of
the dark, she was not where it expected, and she gave up the ground rather than the opening,
falling back a pace and letting it come through the space she had loaned it.

Hrolda did not give up anything. She stepped into it.

It was the wrong call and she knew it half a heartbeat too late, when her boot found the slick of
old rain on worn stone and her whole committed weight went sideways. The Seamfinder was inside her
reach before she finished falling, and it found the gap under her arm, and this time it was not
shallow at all.

She hit the flagstones hard. Lying there with the cold spreading through her side, in the worst
moment of the worst night of the hunt, Hrolda understood something about the way the thing moved
that thirty years of standing in a shield wall had never taught her. It came in on the recovery.
It came in on the step after the swing, every time, and she had just given it the biggest one of
her life.

Something in her lit up like a struck match. She would be spending that later, if there was a
later.

"Hrolda." Wren's voice, low and very close. "Can you hold it for three counts?"

"I can hold it for four."

"Three is plenty." Wren rolled her shoulders, and for once did not hurry. This was not going to be
a quick strike, one of the flicking little cuts that had kept her alive across four cities. This
was going to be the slow one, the committed one, the kind you only get to make when someone else
is willing to be the thing it swings at. "Get its weight onto its front foot. I'll open it up."

Hrolda planted her shield, put her back against the ancient wall of the stonecutters' quarter so
that nothing could ever come at her seams from behind again, and made herself into a door.

The Seamfinder came.
