**Contents**

**1. Welcome to STARWROUGHT**

STARWROUGHT is a d20 fantasy roleplaying game with no classes. There is
no wizard, no fighter, no rogue. There is only what your character has
actually done, recorded in talent constellations. Fight with two axes
and the Two-Weapon Fighting constellation opens to you. Slip past a
death cult unseen and Stealth beckons. Your character sheet is a map of
your deeds.

If you know Pathfinder Second Edition, you already know most of this
game: three actions, four degrees of success, level added to nearly
everything. What changes is who your character is allowed to become. We
removed the walls.

**The Three Laws**

-   **You are what you do.** No classes. Talent constellations define
    you; attributes emerge from where you invest; play itself decides
    where you can grow.

-   **One currency.** Talent points are the only build resource. No stat
    arrays, no skill increases, no feat slots, no magic-item math.

-   **Earned, not scheduled.** Critical moments at the table Flare your
    constellations. Milestones grant the points. The story writes the
    build.

**What You Need**

A d20 and normal dice, this handbook, and a character sheet. This
playtest covers physical characters: warriors, hunters, thieves,
brawlers. Magic arrives in a later packet. Monsters, hazards, and DCs
from Pathfinder 2E (Remastered) work as printed, so any bestiary on your
shelf is already a STARWROUGHT bestiary.

**Symbols**

**◆** one action • **◆◆** two actions • **◆◆◆** three actions • **◇**
free action • **↺** reaction • **★** capstone talent (requires a Master
talent from its constellation) • **✧** heritage root (granted by your
heritage choice)

**2. The Core Rules**

**Character Mechanics**

STARWROUGHT combines familiar pieces of character mechanics with a few
new ones.

Characters are built from their **ancestry** (elf, dwarf, orc, human),
**heritage** (the lineage within it: a starlit elf, a forge-blood dwarf,
a versatile human), **culture** (where they grew up), and **background**
(how they grew up).

STARWROUGHT has four attributes: **Might, Agility, Wits, and Presence**.
Might is muscle, endurance, and raw force. Agility is speed, balance,
and precision. Wits is perception, memory, and fast thinking. Presence
is willpower, charm, and weight of personality. You never buy them and
never roll them up. Every related talent constellation feeds one
attribute, and your attribute bonus is simply the points you\'ve spent
in its constellations ÷ 5 (round down, minimum +1 if fractional, maximum
+5). Attributes add to every check you make, trained or not, plus HP and
talent prerequisites.

Instead of skills, classes, and feats, everything a character can do
grows on **talent constellations**. Enter any constellation by spending
1 point on a Trained talent; total investment sets your rank in it; rank
unlocks deeper tiers.

Characters still gain **levels**, each four milestones long: milestones
1--3 grant 1 talent point apiece, spent immediately in a Flared
constellation (one your recent deeds have set alight; Chapter 5 has the
whole loop). Points are never banked: you spend each the moment you
receive it. The 4th milestone is the level-up: HP rises, every
level-based check and DC ticks up by 1, and you gain a Comet, a talent
point that answers to no constellation: spend it immediately, anywhere,
Flared or not, opened or not. No talent point is ever banked.

**Checks**

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **d20 + level + attribute + proficiency (if any) + bonuses & penalties**
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------

**One formula for everything.** Adding your **level** is what makes the
game heroic: a tenth-level character clears bars the most gifted
commoner alive never will, in every field at once. Adding your
**attribute** means raw talent always shows up; no one is ever reduced
to a bare d20. **Proficiency** is your rank bonus in the constellation,
and Untrained simply means +0 proficiency. Status, item, and
circumstance bonuses or penalties then apply; bonuses of the same type
never stack, so take the best. This one rule governs skills, attacks,
saves, and armor alike.

**Degrees of Success**

  --------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Result**            **Meaning**
  Beat the DC by 10+    Critical success, and a possible Flare (Ch. 5)
  Meet or beat the DC   Success
  Miss the DC           Failure
  Miss by 10+           Critical failure, and, if it\'s dramatic and/or consequential, a possible Flare
  Natural 20 / 1        Step the result one degree up / down
  --------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Hero Points**

**Start each session with 1 Hero Point** (maximum 3; the GM awards more
for heroism, clever play, and beautiful failure). Spend a Hero Point to
**reroll a check you are Trained or better in**, taking the new result.
You may also spend all your Hero Points (minimum 1) to avoid death: that
isn\'t a roll, so it\'s always allowed.

**Ranks: Points Are Progress**

Every point you spend in a constellation buys a talent, and your running
total in that constellation sets your rank in it. Deep investment
rewards you with proficiency ranks; there is no separate advancement to
track. Talents themselves are tier-gated: an Expert talent requires
Expert rank.

**The first point is always the root.** All constellations open with an
initial talent, their root; buying it is what makes you Trained. Nothing
in a constellation can be bought before its root.

  --------------- --------------------- --------------------------------------- -----------------------------
  **Rank**        **Proficiency**       **Points in constellation to unlock**   **Minimum level to unlock**
  Untrained       +0 (attribute only)   0                                       ---
  **Trained**     +4                    1                                       Level 1
  **Expert**      +7                    4                                       Level 5
  **Master**      +10                   9                                       Level 13
  **Legendary**   +13                   16                                      Level 19
  --------------- --------------------- --------------------------------------- -----------------------------

**The Four Attributes**

**Might, Agility, Wits, Presence.** You never buy them and never roll
them up. Every related talent constellation **feeds** exactly one
attribute, and the points you\'ve spent in its constellations grow to be
that attribute\'s points. Spend enough anywhere in Might-feeding
constellations and you simply are that strong.

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Attribute bonus = points ÷ 5, round down (minimum +1 if fractional • maximum +5)**
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ---------------------- -----------
  **Attribute points**   **Bonus**
  1--9                   +1
  10--14                 +2
  15--19                 +3
  20--24                 +4
  25+                    +5
  ---------------------- -----------

The same points raise rank and attribute together; training and talent
grow from the same deeds. That is the whole idea.

**Derived Statistics**

**Saves are checks.** Fortitude, Reflex, and Will are talent
constellations of their own (feeding Might, Agility, and Presence), so a
save is simply the Checks formula rolled with that constellation.
Everyone starts Trained in all three: their Training roots are granted
free at creation, and their deeper talents are bought like any other.
Awareness Training is granted free as well, so initiative (an Awareness
check) works for everyone from level 1. So is Weapons Training: attack
rolls add the Weapons constellation\'s proficiency, with any weapon.
Initiative works the same way as an Awareness check (your exploration
activity can swap the constellation: Avoiding Notice rolls Stealth).
Weapon damage lives with the weapons in Chapter 4. That leaves very
little to derive:

  ---------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Statistic**    **Formula**
  Hit Points       Ancestry base + (6 + Might) per level. Recompute when Might changes
  Armor Class      10 + level + attribute up to the armor\'s cap + proficiency (if any) + armor bonus
  Any ability DC   10 + level + attribute + proficiency (if any) + bonuses & penalties. Your check with a 10 in place of the die
  ---------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**3. Creating Your Character**

**Your first constellation is yourself.** Ancestry, heritage, and
culture merge into one sky, your **Origin**: the heritage branches left,
the culture branches right, and every Origin point spends anywhere in
it. Culture and background points offer a choice, broad or deep: push
them outward into the Skills those lives teach, or inward into the
Origin itself. Your history literally builds your starting attributes.

![](media/ece02c2453c05105e4081042868c7e9602aa7e87.png){width="6.895833333333333in"
height="1.6979166666666667in"}

  ---------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------------------------------------------------------------
  **Step**               **You choose**                                                                     **Points**
  0\. Starting talents   Everyone begins Trained in Fortitude, Reflex, Will, Awareness, and Weapons         5 roots, granted free
  1\. Ancestry           Your body and blood: HP, Speed, senses; the root founds your Origin, free          1 (anywhere in your Origin)
  2\. Heritage           Your lineage (✧), the left branch of your Origin; its root is granted free         1 (anywhere in your Origin)
  3\. Culture            Where you were raised, the right branch of your Origin; its root is granted free   2 (its two Skills or your Origin, each point broad or deep)
  4\. Background         Your trade; its Lore root is granted free                                          2 (its two Skills or your Origin, each broad or deep)
  5\. Armor point        1 free point in any Armor constellation, Unarmored included                        1 (granted)
  6\. Comets             Talent points that answer to no constellation: anything, anywhere                  3
  ---------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------------------------------------------------------------

**The count:** about 10 spent points, plus nine roots granted free (four
identity, three saves, Awareness, Weapons), the background\'s Lore, and
any talent grants, so a fresh character owns \~20 talents. Every point
is spent the moment you receive it: there is no banking, at creation or
ever. Max 3 bought points per constellation at creation (free identity
roots exempt); everyone is Trained-capped until level 5. Strongly
consider 1 point in an armor constellation: Trained is +4 proficiency on
top of your armor\'s built-in bonus, still the single best point you
will ever spend.

**Ancestries**

Choose an ancestry: its root talent is granted free (your blood pays),
then spend 1 point in its constellation. Heritages live inside the
ancestry constellation, marked ✧ on the star map and listed in their own
table, never among the buyable talents. You can\'t spend a point on a
heritage root: your chosen heritage grants it free at the next step.

**Cultures**

Culture is deliberately separate from ancestry: an orc raised among
Varisians is simply both. Choosing a culture grants its root free
(usually a language and a birthright). Each culture names two Skills its
people grow up practicing; your 2 culture points go into one or both.
The culture constellation\'s own talents are bought with Comets, when
you choose to lean into where you came from.

**Finishing Up**

-   **Attributes:** total your points per attribute (each constellation
    feeds one); bonus = points ÷ 5, round down; minimum +1 if
    fractional, maximum +5.

-   **Derived stats:** HP, AC, saves, initiative from Chapter 2. Note
    your rank (almost always Trained, +5) in each constellation you own.

-   **Gear:** take weapons matching your styles, armor matching your
    armor constellation, and a kit that fits your background. Items
    carry no math yet; take what makes sense and the GM adjudicates.

**4. Playing the Game**

**The Three-Action Round**

On your turn: three actions (◆) and one reaction (↺) per round, spent
however you like: Strike three times (MAP applies), Stride-Strike-Raise
a Shield, whatever the moment demands. Activities like Double Slice
bundle actions for stronger effects. This is pf2e\'s engine, unchanged;
every trick your table knows still works.

  -------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Common actions**         **Effect**
  Strike ◆                   Attack with a weapon or fist (Combat Style rank, or attribute untrained)
  Stride / Step ◆            Move your Speed / move 5 ft without provoking
  Raise a Shield ◆           +2 circumstance AC until your next turn
  Seek ◆                     Awareness check vs. Stealth DC: find hidden creatures and hazards
  Grapple / Shove / Trip ◆   Athletics check vs. Fortitude or Reflex DC
  Demoralize ◆               Intimidation vs. Will DC: frightened 1 (2 on a crit)
  Feint ◆                    Deception vs. Perception DC: off-guard to your next melee attack

**Arms & Armor**

Weapons list dice and traits. Your attack bonus comes from the Weapons
constellation with any of them; how you wield sets the attribute
(Agility for Dueling, Might for Brawling, and so on), and no weapon is
locked to a style. Losing your off-hand blade changes your style, never
your skill at hitting things. Damage is weapon dice + Might +
specialization (Expert +2, Master +3, Legendary +4), with extra dice at
levels 4, 12, and 19 (2, 3, then 4 dice).

**Armor carries two small stats** that always sum to 3: an attribute cap
(how much Agility or Might counts toward your AC) and an armor bonus
(what the armor itself provides). Lighter armor draws that budget from
you; heavier armor draws it from the steel, so plate protects a farmhand
on day one. Once your attribute fills the cap, every armor lands on the
same AC. Choose by speed, story, and talents. The cap touches only AC;
your full attribute still applies to every check. Item bonuses are a
different thing entirely: they come from armor quality, not weight, and
arrive with the equipment packet.

**Shields** are gear plus a Combat Style: anyone can Raise a Shield (+2
AC, ◆), and the Shield Fighting constellation turns it into a fortress.
Armor proficiency only applies while wearing that armor type.

**Flares: Growth You Earned**

A constellation becomes **Flared** by one universal trigger: a
**Critical Success or Critical Failure on a roll with consequences,
directly related to a talent in that constellation**. Any roll at the
table counts, yours or an enemy\'s against you, trained or not. The
constellation\'s talents define its territory, and crit failures count
equally; you learn from disasters, and luck never locks your growth. A
constellation is either Flared or it isn\'t (a checkbox, not a count),
and it stays Flared until you spend into it. If nothing rides on the
roll it can\'t Flare, and it probably shouldn\'t be rolled at all;
consequence-free practice is what downtime training is for. Max one new
Flare per constellation per session. The constellation needn\'t be open,
either: an untrained crit, success or spectacular failure, Flares a
constellation you\'ve never bought into, and the milestone point that
follows can buy its root. Your plans get the level-up point; your deeds
get everything else.

-   **GM Flare awards:** for feats no die roll captured: carrying the
    survivor out of the fire Flares Athletics, no check required.

-   **Downtime training:** a week with a teacher, rival, or manual
    Flares that constellation. This is the deliberate path to growth you
    haven\'t stumbled into at the table.

**Retraining**

A week of downtime moves 1 spent point anywhere legal (prerequisites and
tier gates still apply). Characters are maps of what you\'ve done, but
maps get redrawn.

**Level-Up Checklist**

-   +1 to every roll and DC that includes your level (that\'s most of
    them).

-   +HP equal to 6 + Might. If Might changed this level, recompute your
    whole HP total.

-   Check tier gates (Expert at level 5, Master at 13, Legendary at 19)
    and spend your Comet.

-   Recompute attributes if points moved; new dice at L4/L12/L19 (2/3/4
    weapon dice).

**6. Talent Constellations**

talent from the same constellation. Every constellation lists the
attribute it feeds and its Flare triggers (how play opens it for
